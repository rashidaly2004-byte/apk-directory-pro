Semantic tags are just labeled boxes

August 25, 2026

A page is boxes inside boxes. Semantic tags are boxes with meaningful names. That is honestly 90% of it.

The main regions

<header>, <nav>, <main>, <footer> — the big four that frame nearly every page on the web.

The content groupers

<article> for self-contained pieces, <section> for thematic chunks, <aside> for side notes.

💡 Did you know?

Press F12 (or right-click → Inspect) in your browser to open DevTools and see the structure of ANY page on the internet. Try it on your favorite site — you'll recognize the tags now.

So what are <div> and <span> for?

<div> (block container) and <span> (inline container) mean nothing on purpose. They exist as hooks for CSS styling when no semantic tag fits. The rule of thumb: reach for a semantic tag first, and use div/span only when nothing else applies.
