Lesson 2: Working with Text

Headings come in six levels

<h1> is the most important, <h6> the least. Use them like an outline of a document — don't pick a level just because of its size. Search engines and screen readers use heading levels to understand your page's structure.

This is an h3 — a subsection of the h2 above

This is an h4 — a sub-subsection

You will rarely need h5 and h6.

Making text stand out

Use <em> for emphasis (usually shown as italics) and <strong> for importance (usually shown as bold).

There are also <b> and <i>, which look the same but carry no meaning — prefer em and strong, because they say why the text stands out, not just how it looks.

A few more useful ones: <code> renders text in a monospace font (great for code, like console.log()), <mark> highlights text, <small> makes fine print, <del> shows deleted text and <ins> shows inserted text.

Line breaks

Here is something important: in HTML, pressing Enter in your editor does NOT create a new line on the page. The browser collapses all whitespace (even this!) into single spaces.

To force a line break inside a paragraph, use <br>:

Quoting people

The secret of getting ahead is getting started.

For a short quote inside a sentence, use <q>: my mentor always says ship it, then polish it — the browser adds the quotation marks for you.

Special characters

Since < and > start and end tags, you can't type them directly in your text — the browser would think you're opening a tag. Instead you write ENTITIES:

&lt; shows <  •  &gt; shows >  •  &amp; shows &  •  &nbsp; is a non-breaking space
